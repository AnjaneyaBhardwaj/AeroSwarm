## LLM rationales

- early_blowup, L4: Blowup at iteration 24 (solver_blowup@it24:Ux) is early-stage divergence, and mesh check shows max_aspect=349.45 which is very high despite passing checkMesh ok; combined with high relax_U=0.9 this points to mesh quality (near-wall aspect ratio) rather than pure numerics. Early failures point at mesh/BC per guidance, so re-mesh with thicker first cell and gentler growth (L4) should reduce aspect ratio and improve near-wall stability before trying relaxation or scheme changes.
- early_blowup, L1: Blowup at it23 on p with very early divergence but checkMesh passes (max_aspect 134.9 is high but not failing) and L4 remesh already tried without success. With mesh ruled out, the aggressive relaxation (relax_U 0.9, relax_p 0.5, relax_turb 0.9) is the likely driver of the early solver blowup; lowering relaxation (L1) directly targets this instability before trying scheme or init changes.
- late_divergence, L1: Failure at iteration 100 (not early, mesh check ok: max_non_ortho 38.8, skewness 0.75, aspect 349 all acceptable) with residual_growth signature on omega, and high U/omega/k residual tails, points to numerics rather than mesh. relax_U is currently high at 0.9 (not yet lowered from default per ladder L1), so lowering relaxation (U->0.5, p->0.2, turb->0.5) is the first corrective action per guidance before trying schemes or re-init.
- coarse_surface, L4: Blowup at iteration 20 (Ux) with failure_class 'mesh' indicates an early-time divergence typical of mesh-quality issues rather than numerics. checkMesh passed nominal thresholds but max_aspect=307 is very high, consistent with a too-thin first cell (2e-05) driving near-wall instability at these Re/alpha. Early failures point to mesh/BC per guidance, so re-meshing with a thicker first cell, gentler growth, and more smoothing (L4) directly targets the likely root cause, and no mesh-level action (L4/L5) has been tried yet.
- fast_growth, L4: Blowup at iteration 16 (Ux) falls well before iteration 50, pointing to mesh/BC quality rather than numerics. checkMesh passed nominally but max_aspect=404.6 is extremely high (first_cell=2e-5 at Re=6e6), which is a classic trigger for early solver divergence even when checkMesh 'ok' is true. Re-meshing with a thicker first cell, gentler growth, and more smoothing directly addresses this aspect-ratio-driven instability before trying numerics-only fixes.
- bad_mesh, L4: checkMesh failed (signature checkMesh:3_failed) with severe non-orthogonality (89.93) and skewness (101.16), well above acceptable thresholds. This is a mesh quality failure, not a numerics issue, so L1-L3 (relaxation, schemes, initialization) cannot address it since they don't touch the mesh. Must re-mesh with thicker first cell, gentler growth, and more smoothing to reduce skewness/non-orthogonality before any solve can proceed.

## Findings

Hand-written (session 15, 2026-10-15); `scripts/of_recovery.py report` keeps this section.

1. **Every failing case recovered, under both policies, in at most two levels** (5 cases). The bad mesh, the
   coarse surface (160 cells around) and the fast growth (1.16), the last two genuine failures from the mesh study,
   needed L4 (re-mesh); the late divergence L1 (relaxation); the early blow-up L4 then L1. The LLM agent (6 calls,
   $0.036) chose the same levels as the deterministic policy every time.
2. **The early blow-up is misclassified by the blueprint's rule, and the LLM followed the rule.** Aggressive
   relaxation (U 0.9, p 0.5) blows up at iteration 23-24, which the "before iteration 50 means mesh" rule calls a
   mesh problem: L4 is wasted (the re-meshed case blows up the same way) and L1 fixes it. The failure record shows
   the agent the relaxation settings and its rationale names "relax_U 0.9", yet it re-meshed first, citing the
   rule ("Early failures point at mesh/BC per guidance"). One case; a guidance line such as "a non-default
   relaxation in the setup points at numerics whatever the iteration" would address it (not changed here).
3. **L4 needed stronger smoothing than first written.** Doubling the normal smoothing left the bad-mesh case at
   90 deg non-orthogonality (it failed checkMesh at L4 and the deterministic policy then wasted L1-L3 on a mesh
   failure). Now L4 smooths 8x (max 42 deg on this and the default shapes) and code allows only L4 or L5 after a
   checkMesh failure (L1-L3 do not touch the mesh), for the agent and the policy alike.
4. **Part 1's crashes no longer happen.** The setups that crashed in part 1 (first cell 5e-6 c, 300 c far field)
   converge at L0 since k and omega use first-order convection and p uses PCG; so do first cell 2e-6 c, a 500 c
   far field, and the witness design at alpha 14. The remaining failure sources are injected setups and meshes.
5. **A recovered result carries L4's coarser far field.** L4 shrinks the far field to 50 c; the mesh study shows
   that adds about 9% to Cd at Re 6e6 (NACA 0012) and about 3% at Re 3e5. The ledger records the level
   (`solver_level`), so a recovered of2d result is identifiable.
